# Lantai 兰台

Much of our work now goes to agents: building products, chasing down problems, drafting emails, running experiments, writing docs. We explain the problem, and an agent gets it done. By evening, most of the day's work lives in conversations.

Agents can produce excellent results for us, but they can't live the day for us. What we did outside those sessions, whether things really worked out, what we made of it all: only we know.

Lantai puts both into one journal. It reads the sessions that agents such as Claude Code and Codex CLI leave on your machine, and a model drafts daily reports and rolls them up into weekly and monthly ones, every point linked to its conversation. Beside them, you write what no session holds: a thought about the day, a note on a project, a comment on a point the agent made. The two hands stay distinct, and regenerating a report never touches what you wrote. Your data stays on your Mac: Lantai goes online only to send redacted material to the model you chose, and to look for a new version on GitHub when you ask it to.

A record like this may be of little use to the agents, and of little use to the projects. But to us, as people, it matters. Looking back over the days, weeks and months, you see how much you got done working alongside agents, including things you never thought you could do. That meaning is yours. Lantai is also an experiment in how people and agents work together, and how they keep a record together.

Lantai (兰台) was where the Han dynasty court kept its books and archives; the historian Ban Gu worked there, collating texts and compiling the history of his age. Lan (兰) is the orchid: quiet and understated, with a fragrance of its own. Gathering each day's scattered work, putting it in order and filing it away is exactly what this app sets out to do. "Lantai" is its pinyin spelling.
